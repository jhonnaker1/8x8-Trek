                        U.S.S. LEXINGTON  RCB-92
                          MISSION BRIEFING

  Welcome aboard, Captain. Your commission is confirmed and the ship is
  yours: the Lexington, a heavy Research/Battle Cruiser of the Class IX
  line, fleet designation RCB-92.

  She is fully crewed and battle ready. Four hundred and thirty souls
  are aboard -- 43 officers and 387 enlisted -- and every one of them
  is now your responsibility.

  Before you take her out, Command has asked me to run through four
  things: how the war stands, what this ship is, what you will be
  looking at on the console, and every order you can give from it.

  Press RETURN to go on. Press Q at any point and I will get out of
  your way.

                        INTELLIGENCE REPORT

  The Mongol Empire has declared war on the Union. The Vandals have
  since thrown in with them -- not everywhere, and not in strength,
  but their ships are in this sector and they are best left alone.

  Your orders are to clear a sixty-four quadrant section of the galaxy
  of Mongol forces. That is the mission, and you will be judged on it.
  You may be asked to take on rescue work along the way; take it if you
  can, but do not let it cost you the sector.

  We know Mongol ships are here. We do not know where. The same is true
  of our own StarBases -- several were assigned to this sector and are
  still moving into position, so no one at Command can tell you today
  where they will be tomorrow. You will have to find both yourself.

  Scan as you go. An unscanned quadrant is a quadrant you are walking
  into blind.

  Nine panels, all live, all the time. You will not need to ask for
  any of them.

     SHORT RANGE SCAN   the eight-by-eight sectors of this quadrant
     STATUS             stardate, energy, impulse, shields, torpedoes,
                        warp speed, and Mongols left in the sector
     CHART OF KNOWN     every quadrant you have scanned, as a three

     COMMAND            where you are, and the prompt

     SYSTEMS STATUS     the state of repair of all twelve systems
     SHIP'S BADGE      her name, registry and crest

  Messages arrive down the right-hand side, newest at the bottom.

                        NAVIGATION

  The galaxy is 64 quadrants, eight by eight. Each quadrant is 64
  sectors, eight by eight. Warp engines move you between quadrants;
  impulse engines move you within one.

  MOVE      Give the quadrant and the sector you want, vertical first:
            6,2,3,5 takes you to sector 3,5 of quadrant 6,2. Two
            numbers alone are a sector in this quadrant -- 3,5. You
            may type it on the command line as m6235 or m35.

  WARP      Sets the speed the engines will use between quadrants,
            from 1 to 10, and w9 works on the command line. Speed
            costs energy sharply: a fast crossing is an expensive one.

  DOCK      Refuel and repair alongside a StarBase. You must be in a
            sector directly adjacent to it. Visit them often.

                        ENGINEERING

  Everything aboard runs on the Space Industries EnergyConverter. It
  makes 400 units a day when it is undamaged and proportionally less
  when it is not, so a wrecked converter cannot dig you out of a hole.

  ENERGY    Move energy between the three pools -- main, impulse and
            shields -- as you need it.

  MAX       Fill the shield pool from main energy in one order.

  REPAIR    A report on all twelve systems, with an estimated time to
            repair for anything damaged. It costs you nothing.

  FIX       Put the engineering crew on one system instead of spreading
            them across everything damaged, or simply spend time making
            repairs. Time is the price.

                        WEAPONS

  Two systems, and they are good at opposite ranges.

  TORPEDO   Three tubes. One energy torpedo will destroy a standard
            Mongol ship outright, and they are devastating close in.
            You carry a limited number and cannot manufacture more --
            a StarBase or a supply depot is the only way to reload.
            The tube officer will ask how many to fire and where.
            t35 fires one at sector 3,5 without the questions.

  LASERS    Your general-purpose weapon, and there is no ammunition to
            run out of. Damage falls off with range, so lasers reward
            closing. The banks overheat when worked hard and lose
            efficiency until they cool. The laser officer will ask how
            much energy to put into the shot.

                        DEFENCE

  The shields will stop a great deal, but they are not free and they
  are not always the right answer.

  Raising them draws energy from the main banks, so do not raise them
  out of habit. Travelling at warp with the shields up costs double
  the energy of the same trip with them down. Firing a torpedo through
  your own shields throws it off. And the shield generators wear as
  they work: what stops a hit is also damaged by it.

  SHUP      Shields up. The ship on the short range scan turns yellow.
            The up arrow does the same thing.

  SHDN      Shields down, and the ship returns to white. The down
            arrow does the same thing.

                        SCANNERS AND THE CHART

  Short range scanners run continuously and fill the scan panel with
  this quadrant. Damage costs you resolution before it costs you the
  panel: below 90 per cent you will lose the smaller objects and see
  only stars, and below 50 per cent you see nothing at all.

  Long range scanning happens as you travel and writes what it finds
  into the chart, which is on screen permanently. Each scanned
  quadrant reads as three digits:

           the number of Mongol ships
           the type of base -- 1 StarBase, 2 research, 3 supply
           the number of stars

  An unscanned quadrant shows as dots. A quadrant whose star has gone
  supernova is marked and is no longer worth your time.

                        THE SHIP'S RECORDS

  Three orders read out of the computer rather than the scanners.

  CHART     Brings up the full chart of the known galaxy.

  PLAN      Lists every planet found so far, with its quadrant, its
            class and its name. Only quadrants you have scanned are
            listed -- the list cannot know more than the chart does.

  INFO      The command list, when you want reminding.

  All three depend on the computer. Automatic navigation needs it at
  full repair, and a badly damaged computer will lose parts of the
  chart outright -- entries you will have to go back and scan again.
  Repair it before you rely on it.

                        COMMUNICATIONS

  Messages arrive constantly, from your own departments and from
  outside. They stack down the right of the console, newest at the
  bottom, and older ones scroll away as new ones come in.

  MSGS      The message log, further back than the console shows.

  ACK       Acknowledge and clear a message. A2 clears the second one
            down; A on its own clears them all.

  HAIL      Call a StarBase. The immediate answer only tells you the
            frequencies are open -- the base itself replies later, and
            the further away it is the longer you wait.

  SAVE      Write the game to disk.  SND toggles sound.
  QUIT      Leave.  SELF is the self-destruct, if it comes to that.

                        EXPLORATION

  The Lexington was built for exploration and the fittings are still
  aboard. There is no time for survey work with the Union under
  attack, but the planets in this sector hold things worth having --
  energium crystals above all, which will refill your energy banks.

  ORBIT     Enter standard orbit around a planet in this quadrant.

  LAND      Send a party down, by transporter or by shuttlecraft.
            Both need their system at full repair, and neither will
            launch with the shields up. Landing parties can be
            attacked. Some of them do not come back.

  USE       Put anything you have recovered to use.

  That is the briefing, Captain. The sector is yours. Good hunting.
